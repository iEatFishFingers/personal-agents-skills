# Writing role scripts

A good cast is a small number of roles that **collide on purpose**, each with a goal, a character and a short script per period. Scripts are per period (usually a week) so the lead can check the data between rounds.

## What each script contains
- **Role id and name** (used for folders, logs and accounts).
- **Character** in one line (what they want and how they behave).
- **Per period:** 1–3 concrete actions. Include one **boundary probe** (a limit, an edge of a window, a cut-off).
- **The premise** for each period: what must already be true for the action to make sense after the data shift ("your booking from last period is now in the past"). Check each premise against the post-shift data before sending.

## Coverage checklist (cast of about 10)
| Role | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| Regular, subscribed | The intended path over time; subscription rules and expiry |
| Premium or top-tier | Whether the top tier is really different and what it includes |
| One-off, no subscription | Pricing and confirmation clarity; nothing member-only leaking |
| Changes their mind | Cancel, rebook, reschedule; limits that count only live records |
| Limit hitter, two tabs | Caps and races; messages when refused |
| Competes for scarce things | Slot or stock collisions; lists that go stale |
| Never shows or never pays | Outcomes staff must record; consequences (none?) |
| Leaves and deletes the account | Deletion promises vs data kept; sign-in afterwards |
| Staff who works the day | Records outcomes, adds walk-ins, handles phone cancellations |
| Owner who changes configuration | Hours, prices, time off, availability mid-run; handling clashes; month-end review |

## Staff scripts: what to include
- Close out last period's records correctly (completed vs missed), including ones the staff can't truly know.
- Do something staff do under pressure: a walk-in right now, a phone cancellation found by search.
- Make a configuration change that **conflicts with existing records** (time off over bookings, a price change with open orders) and handle the fallout. Say whether customers can know.
- Try an action that should be refused (a future record marked done, a stale tab acting on a changed record) and report the message.
- Month end: walk every record, check statuses, totals and audit lines, restore any configuration the run changed.

## Examples for other domains

**Booking or appointments (barber, clinic, studio):** the table above, with slots as the scarce resource and time off, hours and no-shows as the staff-side stressors.

**Online shop:** regular buyer, discount-code hunter, someone who abandons a basket and returns, someone who tries to buy the last unit from two tabs, a returner who asks for a refund, a customer who deletes their account; staff who fulfil orders, an owner who changes a price while baskets are open and runs a stock count; collision on the last unit; check totals at the time of purchase, stock never negative, refunds reverse the right amounts.

**Ticketing or queue (events, support desk):** a buyer for a limited release, a group buyer hitting the per-person cap, a refunder, a latecomer, a no-show; staff who check people in and handle a double-sold seat; an organiser who changes capacity or price; collision on the last seats; check capacity never exceeded and refund and transfer rules.

**Membership or subscription site:** joiner, upgrader and downgrader, someone who cancels and tries to rejoin, someone who abuses a free allowance, someone whose subscription expires mid-run; staff who handle disputes; an owner who changes plan prices. Check that the plan's benefit really differs, that cancelling does what the page says, and what the economics look like if every member uses the allowance fully.

## Tips
- Share targets deliberately (the same busy slots, the same last item) so collisions happen without you arranging them.
- Give two roles opposite goals (one cancels what the other books).
- Keep scripts short. Agents improvise better from a goal than from a long list.
- Put a **deadline premise** in at least one role (a record that falls inside a cut-off in a later period), so the cut-off rule is tested as the data moves.
